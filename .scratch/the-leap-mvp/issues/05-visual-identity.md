## Question

What should The Leap's logo, color system, and light/dark mode treatment actually be? The white paper sketches a direction (line-form lifted-step mark, sunrise orange/gold, deep navy dark mode, warm white light mode) but the user asked, when this map was charted, for a genuinely fitting concept rather than a literal transcription of that sketch or of the attached mockups' running-figure/footprint marks.

## Answer

Resolved as a prototype, published here: [The Leap Identity](https://claude.ai/code/artifact/86f49123-602a-4acb-9919-b4f4ec7ce665)

**Mark**: three ascending steps that break into a single leaping, arrowed stroke — keeps the white paper's "step" idea but drops its literal-foot/running-figure reading (which reads as a fitness-app mark) and avoids religious iconography (cross, dove, open book) the white paper explicitly wants this brand to avoid. Reads clearly down to favicon size.

**Palette**: Leap orange `#DD7327` (mark, links, active nav) and Ember `#D9622B` (primary CTAs) as the two warm accents, Sunrise gold `#C98A1F` / `#E7B44E` (dark mode) for progress/highlights, warm white `#FBF7F0` for light-mode ground, deep navy `#10141F` for dark-mode ground. The accent hues hold constant across both modes; only the surfaces and text invert.

**Type**: Sora (display/headings) paired with Karla (body) — avoids the Inter/Space-Grotesk default pairing — plus JetBrains Mono for spec labels only, never prose.

Full rationale, at-scale proof (72px/32px/18px), and an applied Home-screen mock are on the artifact page itself.
